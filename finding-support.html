Getting support

Find help that fits you

Explore professional care and peer support on your own terms.

Talk with a qualified professional

A speech-language pathologist or speech-language therapist can assess stuttering and discuss your goals. Ask about their experience with people who stutter in your age group and the languages you use.

Questions for a first appointment

  • How will we choose goals together?
  • Can we work on participation, tension, avoidance, or confidence as well as speech?
  • What are the fees, availability, and online appointment options?
  • How will we review whether therapy is helping me?

Find community

Peer groups can offer shared experiences and connection. Check age suitability, moderation, privacy, cost, and meeting times before joining. Peer support complements professional care.

For parents and carers

If you are concerned about a child's speech, seek advice from a qualified speech-language professional. An assessment can help you understand the child's needs and discuss support without waiting for distress to grow.

Educational information, not individualized treatment. Stuttering support is personal; a qualified speech-language professional can help you choose approaches that fit your needs.
