Responding to teasing and speech-based bullying

Plan support, preserve useful details, and choose a response that feels safe.

Fluent Minds educational guide · Updated 18 November 2026

Practical education and planning ideas, not individual treatment. These guides have not been clinically reviewed. A qualified speech-language professional can help you choose support that fits your needs.

You do not have to manage it alone

Mocking someone’s speech is a problem to address, even when the person calls it a joke. Decide who can help: a trusted teacher, parent, club organiser, supervisor, or student welfare contact. Tell them what happened and what you need next.

You can leave an interaction or ask a friend to stay with you. You do not have to confront someone, explain stuttering publicly, or deliver a clever reply to make your concern valid.

Keep a factual record if useful

Write the date, place, what was said or done, and any witnesses. For online harassment, save relevant screenshots if doing so is safe, then use the platform’s reporting and blocking controls. Avoid repeatedly rereading hurtful messages.

Share the record with the person handling your report. Ask who will see it, what the next step is, and when you can expect an update. Follow your school or organisation’s reporting route and ask for help if you cannot find it.

Choose your own words and boundaries

If you want a short response, try, “Please do not imitate my speech,” or “I want to finish what I was saying.” You may also say nothing and move toward support. Different situations call for different choices.

Afterward, spend time with someone who treats you with respect. If the experience keeps affecting your sleep, studies, or willingness to participate, consider talking with a qualified mental health professional. You deserve support with the impact as well as a response to the behaviour.
