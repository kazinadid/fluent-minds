Questions to ask about online speech therapy

Check suitability, privacy, costs, and the practical setup before booking remotely.

Fluent Minds educational guide · Updated 18 November 2026

Practical education and planning ideas, not individual treatment. These guides have not been clinically reviewed. A qualified speech-language professional can help you choose support that fits your needs.

Confirm the service before paying

Ask whether the provider offers remote appointments for someone of your age, location, and language background. A website mentioning online services does not necessarily mean that every clinician or programme is available to you.

Explain that you are seeking support for stuttering. Ask about the clinician’s qualifications and experience, the assessment process, fees, payment route, cancellation policy, and what happens if the connection fails.

Make privacy part of the conversation

Ask which platform will be used, whether sessions are recorded, where records are kept, and how you will receive documents. If you share a home, discuss a realistic way to get privacy. Headphones may help with listening, but they do not stop someone nearby from hearing your voice.

Agree on how a parent or supporter may join when appropriate. You should know who is present at both ends of the appointment.

Test the basics and keep a backup

  • Check the microphone, speaker, and connection.
  • Keep a charger and a way to contact reception nearby.
  • Put your questions where you can see them.
  • Agree whether a dropped call will be resumed or rescheduled.

After the first appointment, consider whether you could explain your concerns and understood the plan. Discuss any audio delays or accessibility needs with the therapist. This checklist helps you ask informed questions; it does not establish that remote care is suitable for every person or every clinical need.
